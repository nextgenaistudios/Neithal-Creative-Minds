A studio named after the shoreline

Where the land runs out of ideas, the sea keeps sending more.

Why "Neithal"

In Sangam-era Tamil poetry, the land was divided into five thinai — landscapes, each with its own mood and meaning. Neithal was the coast: the seashore, the fishing villages, the tide that never fully arrives or fully leaves.

We named the studio after it because that's how we think about brand work. A promotional film or a digital campaign isn't a fixed object you finish once. It's tidal — it needs to keep moving, keep returning, keep reaching a little further up the shore each time.

A brand that stops moving is a brand the tide has already passed.

That's the standard we hold our own work to: video and digital campaigns built to travel, not sit still in a folder.

What we believe

  • Rooted in story, not templates

    Every brief starts with what makes this brand true, not with a format we already had lying around.

  • Craft over shortcuts

    We'd rather take an extra day on a script or a cut than ship something that only looks finished.

  • One roof, two crafts

    The people who shoot your film also plan where it runs — so the story and the strategy are never handed off cold.

  • We move at your pace

    Small businesses and growing brands don't need agency bureaucracy — they need fast, direct collaboration.

How a project runs

Three stages, start to finish.

1. Discover

We learn the brand, the audience and the goal before a camera or ad account is touched.

2. Create

Scripting, shooting, editing and campaign build-out, with check-ins at every stage.

3. Launch

We publish, run and monitor the work, then report back on what it actually moved.
